The Problem

The Operational Reality in Most Compliance Teams

Across mid-market NBFCs, fintechs, digital lenders, and co-operative banks, the same pattern repeats — investigation-critical workflows running on tools that were never built for compliance.

📊

Unstructured Spreadsheets as Case Management

Shared spreadsheets with hundreds of rows — no access control, no version history, no audit trail. One analyst updates a record, another overwrites it. When the regulator asks who made a specific compliance decision, the answer is: no record exists.

Critical Risk
📱

Informal Channels for Case Collaboration

Case evidence shared over unencrypted messaging apps, compliance decisions undocumented in chat threads, sensitive documents distributed without access controls. When the regulator asks for a documented decision trail, there is none.

Regulatory Exposure
⏰

Regulatory Deadlines Tracked by Individual Reminders

FIU-IND requires STR filing within 7 days of suspicion formation. Teams rely on individual calendar reminders with no systemic oversight. One analyst on leave — the filing window is missed. One missed filing — regulatory penalty, minimum.

Compliance Risk
🧠

Institutional Knowledge Lost at Every Attrition Event

An experienced analyst exits. Dozens of active investigations exist only in their memory or undocumented personal notes. The successor starts from zero. Critical investigation context — relationships, risk signals, decision rationale — is permanently lost.

Operational Risk
🔍

Regulatory Audit Preparation Takes Weeks

When a regulatory examination is announced, the compliance team spends weeks reconstructing an evidence trail — pulling fragments from email threads, chat histories, disconnected spreadsheets, and unstructured documents. This exercise repeats with every audit cycle.

High Cost
💸

Enterprise Platforms Are Priced Out of Reach

Enterprise-grade AML platforms that address these gaps cost ₹2–5 Crore per year in licensing, require 6–12 months to implement, and demand a dedicated vendor support team to operate. The mid-market has had no viable alternative — until now.
